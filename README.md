*Dr. Regan Andrews is an independent researcher based in New Zealand whose work integrates **artificial intelligence, computing, and the study of time**. He holds a Doctor of Computing (DComp) -- a terminal degree equivalent to a PhD -- and investigates how paradoxes, causality, and retroactive change can be modeled computationally. His current research develops algorithmic frameworks for **paradox-aware reinforcement learning, chrono-computing in temporal data systems, and spiral models of time** that formalize recurrence-with-difference. These projects apply AI and computer science methods to classical problems of time physics and philosophy, with applications to closed timelike curves, distributed ledgers, and retroactive knowledge systems. His interdisciplinary approach draws on **theoretical computer science, machine learning, temporal logic, and dynamical systems**, with the goal of reframing paradox as a computable phenomenon.*

## 🧠 Current Research Focus

My research focuses on developing computational frameworks for modeling time, paradox, and reflexivity in data-driven systems. Current areas of investigation include:

- **Paradox-Aware Reinforcement Learning (PA-RL):** algorithms that adapt to closed-loop environments where agent actions reshape future data distributions.
- **Chrono-Computing:** methods for paradox minimization in temporal graphs, retroactive databases, and distributed ledger technologies.
- **Spiral Resolution Framework:** mathematical and algorithmic models of time as recurrence-with-difference, providing iterative stability in paradoxical systems.
- **Applications:** testing these approaches in reinforcement learning, blockchain consensus, retroactive information systems, and AI safety.

While physics has long debated the possibility of time travel, causality loops, and retro-causality, the computational dimension of these problems is only beginning to be explored. Chrono-Computing seeks to model paradoxes as computable phenomena, develop algorithms for their resolution, and build simulation engines that test the stability of time-looped systems. Topics of interest include:
    - Computational models of closed time-like curves (CTCs)
    - AI methods for paradox resolution in temporal graphs
    - Retro-causality and information flow
    - Reinforcement learning in retroactive environments
    - Temporal logics and spiral operators
    - Quantum computing simulations of time travel models
    - Applied chrono-systems in blockchains, databases, and law
